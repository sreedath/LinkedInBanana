Core computer vision models you should know

The evolution of computer vision shows how transformers reshaped every domain. I have been mapping out the core computer vision algorithms and the pattern is striking. We went from highly specialized architectures to transformer-based solutions dominating across all tasks.

Look at the timeline: Classification had AlexNet, VGG, ResNet - each optimized for image recognition. Object detection evolved through R-CNN variants and YOLO. Segmentation developed FCN, U-Net, DeepLab for pixel-level tasks. These were all domain-specific architectures.

Then transformers arrived. Vision Transformer (ViT) showed that the same architecture handling language could work for images. Swin Transformer made it more efficient. ConvNeXt proved that even CNN designs could benefit from transformer insights.

Now we see transformer-based models across every domain. DETR for detection, SegFormer and SAM for segmentation, even generative models like Stable Diffusion and DALL-E use transformer components. The red dots show this shift clearly.

What is interesting is that transformers didn't just match existing performance - they often exceeded it while being more general. The same architecture that generates text can now detect objects, segment images, and create realistic photos.

The post-2020 era (purple dots) shows this acceleration. NeRF revolutionized 3D reconstruction, SAM made segmentation accessible to everyone, and diffusion models changed how we think about image generation.

This convergence toward unified architectures is powerful. Instead of maintaining separate codebases and expertise for each vision task, teams can leverage shared transformer knowledge across domains.

Computer vision has moved from task-specific engineering to general intelligence that adapts to visual tasks.
